Explain in simple languageAll 108 quarter-stars (pādas) of the zodiac, what each one is associated with, and how each planet is said to behave there.

What this page is

The zodiac has 27 stars (nakṣatras), each split into four quarters — 108 in all. For each quarter this page lists its divisional sign, ruling planet, presiding deity, symbol, temperament (gana) and naming syllable, and then how each of the nine planets is traditionally described when it sits in that quarter.

How to use it

Use the search box or the filters (ruling planet, temperament, navāṃśa sign, house) to find a quarter; tick Vargottama only to see the quarters whose navāṃśa sign matches their own sign, and press Reset to clear the filters. The planet buttons (All 9, Sun, Moon…) mark where those planets of your saved chart sit; Hide removes the marks. Cast your chart on the Kundli page first for these marks to appear.

How to read what you see

Read each description as a traditional association, a theme to consider alongside the rest of the chart.

A word of care

These descriptions are tendencies from tradition, not predictions.

Jyotish Reference · BPHS structure, classical symbolism & a derived graha grid

The 108 Nakshatra Padas & the Nine Grahas

Every pada — its navamsa, lord, deity, symbol, gana and syllable — plus, for each, how all nine planets tend to behave when placed there.

● Structure — BPHS

27×4 padas, pada = navamsa (D9), spans, Vimshottari lords, deities. Also the exaltation & natural-friendship tables the planet grid uses. (Vol. 1 Ch. 3–6; Vol. 2 Ch. 46.)

● Symbolism — classical, not BPHS

Symbols, gana, nakshatra character, naming syllables — stable across Brihat Jataka & Phaladeepika, but outside the BPHS verses.

● Graha grid — derived, not quoted

No classical text fixes a verdict for all 972 planet×pada cells. Each is synthesised from: planet karaka × its D1 rasi dignity (primary), refined by its D9 dignity × nakshatra theme × dispositor. Reproducible, not a citation.

● Read as tendencies

These lean toward a direction; they are not fortunes. Judge always with the whole chart, strength & dasha. Nodes have no sign dignity — read via dispositor, with extra caution.

Planet readings
How to read a graha cell: the first chip is the planet's dignity in the rasi (D1 sign) this pada actually falls in — the primary strength (exalted / own / friend / neutral / enemy / debilitated). The smaller D9 chip is its dignity in the pada's navamsa — a refinement. Then the planet's significations coloured by the nakshatra, the dispositor (star-lord) relationship, and a one-line synthesis of the D1+D9 verdict. The graha grid — exactly how each cell is built

For a classical planet in a pada, dignity is judged first from the rasi — the D1 sign the pada physically occupies — using BPHS exaltation/debilitation, own-sign and natural-friendship rules (Ch. 3). This is the first-order strength: e.g. Saturn at Vishakha pada 3 falls in Libra, where Saturn is exalted, even though that pada's navamsa is a Mercury sign and its nakshatra lord is Jupiter. The pada's navamsa (D9) dignity is then read as a refinement, and a short synthesis states whether the two levels agree (vargottama), reinforce, or pull against each other. The karaka themes are filtered through the nakshatra's character, and the dispositor (the nakshatra's Vimshottari lord) is checked as friend/enemy/neutral. Rahu and Ketu have no fixed sign dignity, so their cells carry no verdict — they take the tone of their D1 dispositor (the rasi lord), with a note where tradition assigns them a debated exaltation, as BPHS counsels extra caution with the nodes.

This composition is my educational synthesis. It is not a quotation from BPHS or any other text, and different authors would phrase the blend differently. Treat each cell as a first-order tendency to test against the full chart, not a verdict.

The bhava (house) layer

Pick a house and each planet cell gains a second line. House significations follow BPHS (bhava chapters, Vol. 1 Ch. 11 onward). The planet's tendency in that house is derived from its karaka nature, the house's significations, and the house's functional class — kendra, trikona, dusthana, maraka, upachaya — plus dig bala (directional strength: Sun/Mars in the 10th, Moon/Venus in the 4th, Mercury/Jupiter in the 1st, Saturn in the 7th), all BPHS concepts. The house depends on the lagna, so the same planet-in-pada sits in different bhavas for different ascendants — treat this as a general tendency to place against a real chart. Pada (intrinsic nature) and bhava (life-area) are independent axes; the full reading is their combination. The house line is also modulated by the planet's rasi dignity for that pada — an exalted planet delivers the house's matters powerfully, a debilitated one is hard-pressed — since a house's results depend heavily on the dignity of the planet in it.
