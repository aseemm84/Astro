A yoga is a named planetary combination with a characteristic result. BPHS and the wider classical tradition catalogue hundreds. The discipline is the same for all of them — know the formation, the classical effect, and the conditions that strengthen, cancel or delay it, and remember that a yoga is a promise that a dasha must later keep.

Handle with care. A yoga's presence alone guarantees nothing. It must be activated by a relevant dasha and supported by overall strength; weak or afflicted, it may barely deliver, or a 'bad' yoga may be cancelled. Never promise wealth, power, marriage, children, illness or death from a yoga in isolation.

Yogakaraka

A Yogakaraka is a single planet that owns both a kendra and a trikona from the Lagna — inherently a raja-yoga-giver, and usually the chart's most beneficial planet.

Solar yogas (from the Sun)

A planet other than the Moon in the 2nd from the Sun gives Vesi, in the 12th Vosi, and in both Ubhayachari — benefics give the good results (eloquence, fame, balance), malefics the reverse.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 38 (Vesi, Vosi, Ubhayachari).

Raja Yogas — combinations for power & status

  • Kendra–Trikona association. When a kendra lord (esp. 4/7/10) and a trikona lord (esp. 5/9) join, aspect each other, or exchange signs, a Raja yoga forms. The 9th+10th link (Dharma-Karmadhipati) is especially prized.
  • Maha Raja Yoga. Lagna lord and 5th lord in exchange, or Atmakaraka + Putrakaraka well placed (in the Lagna, 5th, own/exaltation, in aspect to a benefic).
  • Karakamsa yogas. Benefics in kendras from the Karakamsa; Atmakaraka and Putrakaraka related there.
  • Vipareeta Raja Yoga. The lords of the 6th, 8th and 12th placed in each other's dusthanas — 'harm cancels harm', giving unexpected rise (named Harsha / Sarala / Vimala in later texts).
  • Neechabhanga Raja Yoga. A debilitated planet whose debilitation is cancelled (e.g. its dispositor in a kendra from Lagna/Moon) can rise from low beginnings to prominence.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 39 (Raja Yogas; Maha Raja yoga; Karakamsa/AK+PK) and Ch. 40 (yogas for royal association). Vipareeta and Neechabhanga are classical developments (Phaladeepika/Saravali) grounded in BPHS's dusthana-lord and debilitation-cancellation principles.

Dhana Yogas — combinations for wealth

Wealth flows from the lords of the 2, 5, 9 and 11 (and the Lagna) relating well — by conjunction, aspect or exchange — and holding divisional dignity. Specific patterns: Lakshmi and Chandra-Mangala (above), and strong angular+trinal lords in mutual link.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 41 (Dhana Yogas — the roles of the angular, trinal and wealth-house lords and their divisional dignities).

Parivartana (exchange) yogas

When two planets sit in each other's signs, they exchange strength:

  • Maha Parivartana — exchange between good houses (kendras/trikonas/2/11): a powerful mutual boost.
  • Dainya — exchange involving a dusthana (6/8/12): mixed, often struggle-then-relief.
  • Khala — exchange involving the 3rd: uneven, effort-driven results.
Translator / commentary. Parivartana yogas are codified in Phaladeepika/Saravali; they rest on BPHS's principle that a house-lord carries its house's matters, so an exchange tightly binds the two houses.

Arishta and penury yogas — read with care

Handle with care. BPHS catalogues combinations for hardship, poverty and (for infants) Balarishta. Treat these strictly as classical doctrine — not as predictions of misfortune, illness or death for any person. Balarishta especially has many cancellations (benefic aspects, strong Lagna/Moon) and must never be applied fatalistically.
  • Kemadruma (above) — the chief 'lack' yoga from the Moon, cancelled by any nearby support.
  • Daridra / penury yogas (Ch. 42) — e.g. wealth-house lords fallen in dusthanas, or benefics weak — indicating financial struggle, offset by countervailing strength and Dhana yogas.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 42 (combinations for penury) and the longevity/maraka chapters (Ch. 43–44) — see the Avasthas & Longevity page, where these are handled with full caution.

The judging checklist

For any yoga you spot, run the same five steps:

  1. Formation — are the exact conditions truly met (right lords, right houses, from Lagna or Moon)?
  2. Strength & dignity — are the participating planets strong and dignified, or weak/debilitated/combust?
  3. Varga confirmation — does the yoga hold up in the Navamsa and the relevant divisional chart?
  4. Affliction or cancellation — malefic aspects, dusthana placement, or (for bad yogas) cancellation?
  5. Activation — which dasha/antardasha of the yoga-forming planets brings it to life, and when?
Study note. A yoga is a headline; strength, varga and dasha are the fine print. A textbook Raja yoga formed by weak, afflicted planets in bad vargas may pass almost unnoticed, while a modest combination of strong, well-placed lords activated by a good dasha can lift a whole life.
