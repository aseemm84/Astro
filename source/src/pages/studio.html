How this works, and what it will not do

Every figure in every caption is computed at the moment it is written, from the same ephemeris the chart engine uses, the same festival rules the calendar applies, and the same pañcāṅga the now page prints. Nothing comes from a stored table, so nothing can go stale — and the caption and the image cannot contradict each other, because both are built from one day object.

It writes no predictions. Not “Mercury retrograde will disrupt your communication”. This site does not make deterministic claims about anyone's life, and a caption is not exempt from that for being short. What it writes is what is happening, and what the tradition says the thing is: the rule, the exact span, the number, and the distinction people usually get wrong. Retrograde motion is the clearest example — it is a line-of-sight effect of two bodies passing, not a planet reversing, and saying so is both more accurate and more interesting than a forecast.

The ordering is by how rare the event is and how close: a festival or a station outranks an ordinary Tuesday, and the daily pañcāṅga sits at the bottom as the thing that is always true and always postable. Where a festival rule is one that published almanacs apply differently, the caption says so rather than asserting a single answer — the same discipline the calendar keeps.

The image is deliberately plain: a headline, the numbers under it, the place, the handle. The content is the thing nobody else has, so the design stays out of its way.

Turning one into a video

Several of these cards offer Animate this, which opens the matching scene on the chart page — the retrograde loop for a station, the orrery for an ingress, the tithi wheel for a syzygy. From there, Record this loop writes a video file.

That recorder is not a screen capture. The playhead is advanced by an exact increment per frame, drawn, and handed to the encoder one frame at a time, so the result is constant frame rate however fast or slow the machine happened to be drawing. Nothing is uploaded and no toolchain is involved. The Reels guides switch shows where Instagram will cover the frame with its own caption and buttons, and Safe layout lifts everything clear of that strip.

Why the place is on this page too

Sunrise decides which day a tithi names, and sunrise depends on where you are standing. Two cities can honestly disagree about which day an ekādaśī falls on. Station and ingress times shift with the clock too. So the place is a control here exactly as it is everywhere else on this site, and the caption states which place its numbers were computed for — because a figure without a place is not a figure.
