A reel here is not a template with your data poured into it. It is a question and the computation that answers it: the finder runs over the span you choose, and what the reel shows is what it found. If a span holds four events the reel is four events long, and if it holds none the page says so rather than inventing a rhythm.

How it ends

Every reel closes on what the run actually came to — the counts, the mean interval, whether the figure closed and to within how many degrees. Those are read off the computation, not written in advance.

What the pictures do and don't mean

These are astronomy. The rāśi names around the rim are the sidereal signs this site computes everywhere else, and the figures that emerge — a star, a rose, a whirl of arms — are facts about the ratio of two periods and carry no interpretive weight whatever. Venus draws five arms because five of its synodic periods fall within eight years of ours, which is arithmetic. A reel that drew a pentagram and implied it meant something would be a different and much worse kind of object than this one.

Where a reel applies a traditional rule — the degrees within which a graha counts as asta, hidden in the Sun's glare — it names the rule and whose it is. Where astronomy itself has no sharp line, as with how close a syzygy must be to the node before an eclipse is certain, the reel draws the band and says it is a band.

Everything on this page is computed in your browser from the same ephemeris the charts use. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the page works with no network at all once it has loaded.
