A graha is not good or bad; it is somewhere. The same Maṅgala is exalted in one rāśi and fallen in the one opposite, and everything between those two poles is a graded condition the tradition names. This page draws the whole scheme at once — every graha’s dignity across all 360° — for any moment, with no birth chart required.

The whole dignity scheme, in one pictureअवस्था-पट्टः

Each row is one graha and runs the full circle from 0° Meṣa to 30° Mīna. The colour is what that graha’s condition would be standing there. Everything you need to know about dignity is in this one image: the two poles 180° apart, the own signs, the narrow band of mūlatrikoṇa inside one of them, and the friendships filling the rest.

Why the band is narrow in one placeमूलत्रिकोणम्

Mūlatrikoṇa — literally “root triangle” — is a stretch of degrees within one particular sign where a graha is held to be at its most effective: stronger than merely owning the sign, but not the same as exaltation. It is the only dignity here that is a range of degrees rather than a whole sign, which is why the ribbon carries a thin stripe rather than a full band for it.

Two of the seven have no stripe, and that is a decision rather than an omission. Candra’s mūlatrikoṇa falls in Vṛṣabha and Budha’s in Kanyā — and those are the very signs in which each is exalted. This site’s engine resolves dignity in a fixed order and tests exaltation first, so for those two the whole sign reads as exaltation and the narrower band never surfaces. Classically, exaltation has an exact degree (Candra’s is Vṛṣabha 3°) while sign-level dignity treats the whole rāśi as exalted; the two accounts only collide for these two grahas. The table above marks which rows are affected, and the ribbon shows what the engine actually decides rather than a tidier scheme it does not use.

How fast they actually moveगति-भेदः

The difference in speed between the grahas is the reason a chart is a moment rather than a season. The Moon crosses a whole nakṣatra in about a day; Śani takes two and a half years to cross a rāśi. Drawn on one logarithmic scale, because a linear one would show the Moon and nothing else.

Rāhu and Ketu are not in this schemeछाया-ग्रहौ

The two nodes have no sign rulership in BPHS, and so no own sign, no mūlatrikoṇa, and no friendship scheme of the kind the seven have. They are chāyā grahas — shadow grahas, the two points where the Moon’s path crosses the ecliptic — and later tradition assigns them exaltations and rulerships in several incompatible ways. This site’s engine returns the condition node for both rather than picking one of those traditions, and the ribbon above leaves them out for the same reason. Where they stand and how they move is shown with the others, because that much is simply arithmetic.
