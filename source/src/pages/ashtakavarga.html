Ashtakavarga ('eight-fold division') is a points system that grades every sign for support. Eight contributors — the seven planets plus the Lagna — each award a benefic point (a rekha) to certain signs, counted from themselves and from every other contributor by fixed tables. The totals guide transit timing and confirm strength.

Bindus and rekhas

In a planet's Ashtakavarga an auspicious point is a rekha (a line — a favourable 'sthana') and an unfavourable mark is a bindu (a dot — an inauspicious 'karana'). The rekhas a sign collects measure how supportive that sign is for the matters of that planet.

Source BPHS, Vol. 2, Ch. 66 (the eight contributors; rekha = auspicious sthana, bindu = inauspicious karana; the benefic-place tables for each planet reckoned from itself and from the other seven and the Lagna).

Bhinnashtakavarga & Sarvashtakavarga

Bhinnashtakavarga (BAV) — the individual eight-fold chart of one planet — its rekhas across the twelve signs, each sign scoring 0–8. Read a planet's BAV to see which signs (and thus houses) support it.

Sarvashtakavarga (SAV) — the sum of all seven planets' BAVs in each sign — the chart's overall support map (total ≈ 337 across the zodiac). Used heavily for transits and for ranking the houses.

SAV in a signBroad reading
30 and abovewell-supported — transits here and the houses held tend to go smoothly
about 25–29average / mixed support
25 and belowunder-supported — extra care with transits and the matters of that house
Translator / commentary. The SAV thresholds are a widely-used rule of thumb, not a rigid law — a sign's score weights likelihoods that still depend on the whole chart and the dasha in force.

Kakshas — the fine grid

Each sign is further divided into eight kakshas (sub-portions), each ruled by one of the eight contributors in a fixed order (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, Lagna). A transiting planet gives results according to whether the kaksha it is passing through holds a rekha or a bindu — the basis of fine Kaksha transit prediction.

The three shodhanas (reductions)

Before the figures are used for finer, timing-level prediction, BPHS 'purifies' them in a set order:

  1. Trikona Shodhana — within each trine of signs (Aries-Leo-Sagittarius, etc.) subtract the smallest of the three from all three; if any of the three is zero, no reduction is done. (Ch. 67)
  2. Ekadhipatya Shodhana — reconcile the two signs owned by one planet, by rules that depend on whether each sign is occupied and which holds the larger figure. (Ch. 68)
  3. Pinda Sadhana — multiply each rectified figure by the sign-measure (Rasimana) and, where a planet sits there, the planet-measure (Grahamana), and sum — giving the Pinda used to time when a house's matters prosper or suffer under transit. (Ch. 69)

Source BPHS, Vol. 2, Ch. 67 (Trikona), Ch. 68 (Ekadhipatya), Ch. 69 (Pinda Sadhana), Ch. 70 (applying the Pinda to time a house's results under Saturn's transit), Ch. 71 (a longevity estimate from the Ashtakavarga).

Using Ashtakavarga in practice

  • Transit strength: a slow planet (Saturn, Jupiter) transiting a sign with high SAV — and, finely, a kaksha holding a rekha — tends to give results; over a low-SAV sign, more strain.
  • Confirming a house: compare the SAV of a house's sign and the BAV of its lord — high figures reinforce a promise the rasi chart already makes.
  • Timing: the Pinda (after the shodhanas) is used to pinpoint when a house's matters flourish or are stressed as Saturn transits particular nakshatras/rasis.
Handle with care. Ashtakavarga grades support, not fate. A high score does not promise a good event, nor a low one a bad event — it weights probabilities that remain conditional on the full chart and the dasha in force.
Study note. Start with the Sarvashtakavarga: note which signs (and the houses they hold) score high or low, and watch the slow-planet transits accordingly. Add the BAVs, kakshas, shodhanas and Pinda as you move from gauging general support to timing specific results.
