This page is drawn from measurements, not from claims. Every number on it is produced by a harness in the repository and regenerated by running that harness — there is no figure here that was typed into the prose. Where something has not been measured it says so, because an unmeasured quantity with a number beside it is worse than one without.

Four different kinds of statement. They get confused constantly, and confusing them is how a project ends up quoting a convention as an accuracy. Accuracy is this engine against a sky computed by other people’s code. Precision is how finely this engine converges on its own answer, which says nothing about whether that answer is right. Convention is a choice with no correct value — and it is usually the largest difference against any other source. Unmeasured is unmeasured.

The short answer

Measured against ERFA — the International Astronomical Union’s own reference algorithms — on instants spread across , stratified so every month and every hour of the day appears, and compared at matched Terrestrial Time so that two different ΔT models are not mistaken for two different skies.

An arcsecond, against the divisions it could move

A residual only means something once it is put beside the thing it might change. The scale below is logarithmic because it has to span five orders of magnitude: from the Sun’s two arcseconds to a thirty-degree sign. Every division the tradition uses is a band; every graha’s worst measured error is a dot.

The ṣaṣṭyāṁśa is the first division where a slow planet’s uncertainty becomes a visible fraction of an aṁśa — about a twenty-first. Everything coarser than D27 is untouchable at these residuals, which is the honest reason this site does not fuss about its arcseconds: they are already far finer than the questions being asked of them.

The two star-anchored definitions are themselves mutually incompatible by : Citrā held at 180° and Revatī held at 359°50′ cannot both be true.

What follows from that. If this site and another source disagree about a chart, the ayanāṁśa is about a thousand times more likely to be the reason than anybody’s ephemeris. Check the convention first, and the sunrise definition second.

The daśā figure is the one that matters and the one most easily got wrong. A hundred-and-twenty-year Viṃśottarī cycle maps onto a single nakṣatra of 13°20′, so the conversion is days of daśā per arcsecond of Moon — not per arcminute, and emphatically not the Moon’s daily motion, which gives an answer some five hundred times too small.

For scale: one minute of birth-time error is about of Moon, which is roughly days of daśā drift. The engine’s own contribution is therefore equivalent to about of birth time — real, not negligible, and smaller than the birth time anyone actually has.

Where the engine is checked, and what happens outside it

1800–2200 is the window this engine is checked over, not where it stops working. A chart dated outside it still casts in full and says so at the top of its Overview. What degrades is the Moon — and not the ayanāṁśa, which was the obvious guess and the wrong one.

Past 1000–3000 nothing is quoted at all. The reference used to measure the engine is itself only documented over that range, and a number invented beyond it would be worse than silence.

The two siddhāntic engines

This site also computes the sky the way the Sūrya Siddhānta does, with the text’s own constants — its twenty-four-entry sine table at radius 3438, its epicycles, its revolution counts. That engine lands degrees from the modern one, which is the method’s own accuracy rather than a fault in the implementation.

A second tier keeps every one of those equations exactly as it is and measures the constants — a bīja on each body’s revolutions, an epoch longitude, a scale on each paridhi of the pair the text states, an offset on each mandocca. numbers, every one a quantity the tradition itself treats as correctable. No term is added and no formula changes.

This is not the Sūrya Siddhānta’s answer. It is the text’s method carrying measured numbers, and the bare text stays selectable and unchanged beside it. With the Moon still at about a degree, the measured tier is for study and comparison — not for casting a chart.

Why the Moon will not come down, and how we know

Measuring the constants takes the Sun to about an arcminute and the slow planets to under ten. The Moon, Mars and Mercury barely move. The obvious reading is that their limit is structural rather than parametric — and obvious is not the same as established, so it was measured.

The Moon’s residual, regressed on the arguments of the two largest terms this theory does not have, recovers their textbook amplitudes from the residual alone to under one per cent. Removing just those two terms drops its scatter by a factor of four. The Moon’s limit here is the absence of terms, not the value of numbers — an evection is a function of the elongation from the Sun, and nothing in the parameter set is. No choice of bīja, paridhi or mandocca could ever supply one. Adding them would make this a different astronomy wearing the same parameters, which is the line this tier does not cross.

Precision, which is a different claim

How finely the engine converges on its own answer. None of this says the answer is right; it says the arithmetic is not the limit.

The lagna is worth separating out, because its two figures look contradictory and are not. Given the engine’s own sidereal time and obliquity, its closed form agrees with an independent horizon-plane construction to — exact, at every latitude tested. So the whole of its end-to-end residual is the sidereal-time disagreement seen through the geometry, which near the pole is amplified about ninefold. Testing a construction separately from its inputs is why the polar residual could be attributed rather than merely tolerated.

What has not been measured

The spread between ΔT predictions after 2027. Not measured. The figure the harness prints is very nearly ΔT itself, not the uncertainty in predicting it, and two sources are not enough to measure a prediction spread when one of them is not modelling the quantity. The true lunar node is not pinned by any current harness. Rise, set and twilight are convention-bounded rather than measured against an independent implementation. Jupiter’s and Saturn’s true accuracy is bounded but not attributable — the reference’s own error there is and , so most of the measured disagreement may be the reference’s rather than the engine’s. A JPL kernel would settle it, and is not reachable from the build environment.
