The Shodasha Vargas are sixteen divisional charts. Each splits every sign into equal parts and re-maps them onto signs, producing a finer chart focused on one subject. They refine the rasi chart, never replace it.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 6 (definition and reckoning of each division) and Ch. 7 (the Shad/Sapta/Dasa/Shodasa varga groupings and Vimsopaka strength).

How to read a divisional chart

  1. Establish the D1 promise first. A topic must be indicated in the rasi chart before a varga can develop it — the varga shows the quality and detail, not a promise the D1 never made.
  2. Read the varga's crux house(s) — the house that is the subject (e.g. the 5th in Saptamsa for children, the 10th in Dasamsa for profession) — with its lord, occupants and aspects.
  3. Then read the other houses of that chart as the universal significations refracted into the subject (tables below).
  4. Weigh vargottama and repeated dignity. A planet dignified in D1 and the varga (especially vargottama — same sign in both) is genuinely strong; this is tallied as Vimsopaka Bala (see Strength).
Translator / commentary. BPHS assigns each varga its subject and gives specific rules; reading all twelve houses within each varga is the later analytical extension of the universal house meanings into that subject. The house tables below are presented as that established method, not as explicit BPHS verses. A ★ marks the houses that carry the most weight in each chart.
Study note. Not every varga is read as a full twelve-house chart. D1, D9, D10, D7, D4, D12, D24, D16, D20, D3 and D30/D60 are commonly read as charts; D2 (Hora) is a two-part wealth device; D40/D45/D60 are specialised. Below, chart-type vargas get a house table; device-type vargas get their actual method.

D1 · Rashi — the life as a whole

The base chart. Its twelve houses are the universal significations detailed on the Bhavas page; every other varga refracts these into a subject.

D2 · Hora — wealth & sustenance

A two-part device rather than a twelve-house chart. Each 30° sign is halved into two horas. In an odd sign the first 15° is the Sun's hora (Leo) and the second 15° the Moon's hora (Cancer); in an even sign the order reverses.

  • A planet in the Sun's hora leans toward wealth through effort, enterprise and the paternal/masculine line.
  • A planet in the Moon's hora leans toward wealth through relationships, accrual and the maternal/feminine line.
  • Many benefics in the appropriate hora, and a strong 2nd/11th, indicate sustained wealth.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 6 (Hora reckoning) and Ch. 7 (Hora effects — the three 'male' planets reveal in the Sun's hora, Moon/Venus/Saturn in the Moon's hora).

D30 · Trimsamsa — misfortunes, health & character

Reckoned unusually: no luminaries own Trimsamsas. Each sign's 30° is split among five planets — in odd signs Mars 5°, Saturn 5°, Jupiter 8°, Mercury 7°, Venus 5°; in even signs the order and rulers reverse (Venus, Mercury, Jupiter, Saturn, Mars). Read chiefly for adversity, vices, and health troubles.

D60 · Shashtiamsa — the karmic totality

The subtlest division — half a degree per part. BPHS gives the 60 amsas named deities (e.g. Ghora, Rakshasa, Kaala on the difficult side; Amrita, Poornachandra, Sudha on the benefic side), and the amsa a planet falls in colours its result markedly. It is read as a capstone on all matters — the residual karma behind every house.

Handle with care. D60 is extremely birth-time sensitive — a few minutes' error moves a planet to a different amsa and flips its tone. Rectify the birth time before drawing any conclusion from it; treat it as the last layer of confirmation, not a first read.
Study note. A realistic study order: master D1 and D9 first (they carry most of the weight and are less time-sensitive), then add the one varga that matches your question — D10 for career, D7 for children, D4 for property, D24 for study — and only later the ultra-fine D30/D60 once your birth time is well rectified.
