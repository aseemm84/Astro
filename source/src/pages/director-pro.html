    How this page works

    Studio Pro's Director mode gave a layer an entry, an exit, a continuous motion and somewhere to travel to. Four named behaviours cover a great many reels, and they cannot cover rise, hold for two seconds, drift left, then fall away, because that is five changes of direction on one property and the model holds two. Every tool that has met this problem has reached the same answer, and it is not a fifth behaviour. It is keyframes.

    So here every animatable property of every layer is a track — a list of times, values and easings — and the value at an instant is the eased interpolation between the two keys either side. A property with one key is a constant. A property with none uses the layer's own base value. Nothing else animates anything, which is the point: there is exactly one answer to “why is this moving”, and it is always “because of these keys”.

    The motion presets are still one click, and each one writes keyframes rather than setting a mode the renderer obeys. Apply rise, then drag the second key later, and the rise gets slower — instead of discovering that the preset owns the property and your edit is ignored. A hidden second animation system that silently wins over the visible one is the most confusing thing a tool like this can do, and not having one is worth the extra code.

    What a layer can be, and what it can do

    A layer holds a composition from the library — any of the nodes, drawn by any composer that can draw them — or your own text, a caption band, a stamp, a shape, a rule or a wash. Every one of them can be placed, scaled, rotated, skewed, blurred, glowed, graded, masked with a feathered rectangle or ellipse, and composited with any of the sixteen blend modes. Text additionally arrives by character, by word or by line, with tracking, in eleven animators.

    A scene has a backdrop, a camera that zooms, pans and rolls on its own keyframes, and a transition into the next one — a dissolve, a dip, a push, a wipe, an iris, a zoom blur, a flash or a glitch. The transition is drawn over the join rather than added to it, so putting a dissolve in does not retime every scene after it.

    Getting it out

    The reel records frame by frame rather than in real time: the playhead is set, the frame is drawn, and only then is the frame handed to the encoder. A slow machine makes the export take longer; it does not make the reel shorter and it does not drop a frame. Formats run from a 1080×1920 reel to 4K in either orientation, at 24 to 60 frames a second, with the bitrate under your control. Where the browser offers it, the file is MP4 with H.264, which posts without conversion.

    A soundtrack is a file you choose from your own machine. It is decoded by the browser, routed through a gain node with your fades, and muxed into the recording. It is never uploaded, never fetched, and never leaves the computer.

    Every figure on this page is computed in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and the page works with no network at all once it has loaded.
