Jyotiṣa divides one circle several times over, and the divisions are nested rather than rival. Twelve rāśis, twenty-seven nakṣatras, a hundred and eight pādas — each a cut of the same 360°, each used for a different purpose. This page draws all three at once for any longitude you like, with no birth chart required, and everything on it is computed in your browser as you read.

One circle, cut three waysत्रिविधं विभाजनम्

Drag the pointer. The outer ring is the twelve rāśis of 30° each, the middle ring the twenty-seven nakṣatras of 13°20′, and the inner ring the hundred and eight pādas of 3°20′. A pāda is a quarter of a nakṣatra; nine pādas make one rāśi exactly, which is the fact the navāṁśa rests on.

Why nine pādas to a rāśi mattersनवांश-सम्बन्धः

27 × 4 = 108, and 108 ÷ 12 = 9. So the pāda grid and the navāṁśa grid are the same grid: each pāda is exactly one navāṁśa, and stepping one pāda steps one navāṁśa sign. That is not a coincidence anyone arranged — it follows from quartering a twenty-seventh of a circle — but it is the reason a pāda is worth naming at all.

The twenty-seven carry nine lords, three times roundनक्षत्र-स्वामिनः

The nakṣatra lords are not arbitrary and are not twenty-seven different things: they are the nine Viṃśottarī lords in a fixed order, repeated three times around the circle. Colouring the ring by lord makes the repeat visible at once, and it is the whole mechanism by which a Moon’s position becomes a daśā sequence.

What a pāda of the Moon is worth. One hundred and twenty years of Viṃśottarī map onto a single nakṣatra of 13°20′. A whole nakṣatra is therefore 120 years, a pāda is 30 years, and one arcminute of Moon is about . That is the conversion that makes birth-time accuracy matter, and it is computed here rather than quoted.
