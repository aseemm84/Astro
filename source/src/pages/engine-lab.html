This site carries more than one engine. A modern ephemeris computes every ordinary page; a working Sūrya Siddhānta computes the classical one; and a third tier runs the siddhānta’s own equations on constants measured against a modern sky. This page puts all three to the same questions and measures what their disagreements are actually worth — because an error in arcseconds means nothing until it is converted into the units the work is done in. No birth chart required, and everything below is computed in your browser as you read.

Ask all three the same questionत्रि-प्रश्नः

Arcseconds are the wrong unit for judging an engine, because nobody reads a chart in arcseconds. What a reader wants to know is whether the answer changes — whether the nakṣatra is the same nakṣatra, whether the tithi is the same tithi. So pick the question rather than the body, and the last column says whether the three engines actually disagree about the answer.

How often they agree, over a span you choose

One moment proves nothing — the three engines might happen to agree today and part company tomorrow. So this samples the question across a span and reports the rate. Watch what happens to the tithi: the siddhānta names the same nakṣatra as the modern engine most of the time and the same vāra always, because a weekday is arithmetic on a day count and carries no position at all — but the tithi, which is the difference of two longitudes and so collects both errors, is the one a reader would notice.

What an arcsecond is worthकला-मूल्यम्

This is the conversion that makes the rest of the page meaningful, and it is the one most often skipped. An engine’s accuracy is quoted in arcseconds; a chart is read in rāśis, nakṣatras, pādas, aṁśas and daśā periods. Drag the slider and watch the same quantity restated in every one of those units at once. The markers underneath are where each engine and each body actually sits, measured.

The one that surprises people. Traversing one nakṣatra of 13°20′ traverses that nakṣatra lord’s whole mahādaśā — six years for the Sun, twenty for Venus — so the exchange rate between the Moon’s position and daśā time is lord-specific, not one fixed number. A full circuit of the nine lords is one hundred and twenty years and spans nine nakṣatras, which is one hundred and twenty degrees, giving a cycle average of exactly one year of daśā per degree of Moon. The rate is shown above, computed rather than quoted. It is still steep enough that an error far too small to move the Moon out of its pāda shifts a mahādaśā boundary by a day or two, and one minute of birth-time uncertainty is worth several days of daśā — a larger correction than any engine on this page contributes.

Correction. This note previously said the cycle mapped onto a single nakṣatra, and gave a fortnight per mahādaśā boundary and a month per minute of birth time. Those figures were too large by a factor of between six and twenty — the factor being 120 divided by the lord’s years.

The engines are not the problemसम्प्रदाय-भेदः

Everything above, and every convention this site has to choose between, on one logarithmic ruler from an arcsecond to ten degrees. The ruler is the argument: the gap between the best and worst engine is a matter of arcminutes, while the gap between two defensible conventions — which ayanāṁśa, which anchor star, which definition of sunrise — is measured in degrees. Anyone comparing this site’s output with another almanac will be looking at the right-hand end of this ruler, not the left.

Where the modern engine stops being certifiedसीमा

The modern engine is held to an independent computation of the sky over 1800–2200, and outside that window the honest statement is a disagreement rather than an error, because the reference degrades away from the present too. Drag the year and the band is named, with what it costs the question you chose at the top of the page.

Which engine answers which questionयन्त्र-विभागः

Generated from the measured figures rather than written out, so it cannot drift from them. “Measured” means compared against code other than this project’s; “convention” means a choice with no correct value, which is not an error however large it is.

What none of these engines is. None of them is a prediction, and the difference between two of their columns is not an error in either one. Charts on this site are cast from the modern ephemeris; the fitted tier's Moon sits about sixty arcminutes from it, most of a pāda, and the bare text's several degrees. The siddhāntic tiers are here so that the arithmetic the tradition was built on can be seen working, and so that its distance from a modern computation is a thing you can measure instead of a thing you are told.
