A dasha is a planetary period that times when a chart's promises unfold. BPHS describes many systems — nakshatra (planet) dashas and rasi (sign) dashas. Vimshottari is the default for general use, because the text calls it the most appropriate for this age.

Vimshottari Dasha

A 120-year cycle: nine planets rule in a fixed order for fixed lengths. The opening Mahadasha is set by the Moon's birth nakshatra (Janma Nakshatra).

Order: Ketu → Venus → Sun → Moon → Mars → Rahu → Jupiter → Saturn → Mercury.

Balance of dasha at birth — worked example

  1. Find the Moon's Janma Nakshatra and its lord. Say the Moon is in Rohini — lord Moon, a 10-year Mahadasha.
  2. Find how far the Moon has already crossed that nakshatra. Say it has traversed 40% of Rohini.
  3. Expired dasha = full period × fraction traversed = 10 × 0.40 = 4 years.
  4. Balance at birth = 10 − 4 = 6 years of Moon Mahadasha remaining; then Mars (7y), Rahu (18y), and so on in order.
  5. Compute the fraction from the exact sidereal Moon longitude and the stated nakshatra boundaries; state your ayanamsha (Lahiri by default).

Sub-periods — the proportional rule

Every level nests inside the one above by the same proportion:

Antardasha (Bhukti) — = Mahadasha years × Antar-lord years ÷ 120. The order within a Mahadasha starts with the Mahadasha lord itself, then continues in Vimshottari order.

Worked example. Within Jupiter Mahadasha (16 y), the Saturn Antardasha = 16 × 19 ÷ 120 = 2.53 years (≈ 2 y 6.4 m). The same formula, applied to the antardasha, gives the Pratyantardasha, then Sookshma, then Prana — each a proportional slice of the period above it.

Reading a dasha

Judge the period lord through this order: natural significations → functional role (houses owned from Lagna) → sign & house placement → dignity/strength → associations & aspects → relevant varga → the Mahadasha–Antardasha relationship → the houses activated → and the overall chart promise. Transits are consulted only when asked.

Handle with care. Results are conditional. Avoid blanket verdicts like 'Jupiter dasha is always good' or 'Saturn dasha is always bad' — the outcome depends on the lord's dignity, ownership, placement and strength in this chart.

Conditional nakshatra dashas

BPHS gives several alternative nakshatra dashas, each prescribed only for a chart that meets its condition. Each opens by counting nakshatras from a fixed starting star to the Janma Nakshatra (inclusive) and dividing by the number of lords; the remainder names the opening lord, and the balance is the untraversed part of the Janma Nakshatra, as in Vimshottari. Ashtottari is the most commonly encountered.

Kala, Chakra, Kalachakra and the rasi (sign) dashas

Sign dashas move through signs rather than planets. Most count each sign's years from the sign to its lord (the Chara years) or by its modality (the Sthira years: movable 7, fixed 8, dual 9).

Study note. Get fluent in Vimshottari first — the balance-at-birth arithmetic and the antardasha proportion cover most practical timing. Add Ashtottari and the Chara rasi dasha once the Vimshottari reading feels natural, and always confirm a dasha's promise against the chart before timing an event.
