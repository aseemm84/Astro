BPHS treats nine grahas. Seven are the classical planets from the Sun to Saturn; Rahu and Ketu are the lunar nodes — shadow grahas, mathematical points where the Moon crosses the ecliptic, recognised as grahas for delineation though they are not luminous bodies.

Source BPHS, Vol. 1, Ch. 3 (planetary characters, castes, gunas, elements, sexes, seasons, dhatu/moola/jeeva); karaka significations recur across Ch. 3, the bhava chapters, and the dasha chapters.

Natural benefics and malefics

A graha's naisargika (natural) status is intrinsic and never changes — distinct from its functional role for a given Lagna, which depends on the houses it owns (see Bhavas).

Natural benefics (Saumya)Natural malefics (Krura / Papa)
Jupiter; Venus; the waxing (bright) Moon; unafflicted MercurySun; Mars; Saturn; the waning (dark) Moon; Mercury with malefics; Rahu; Ketu
  • Mercury takes the character of its company — benefic when alone or with benefics, malefic with malefics.
  • The Moon is benefic when bright (roughly the eight tithis around the full Moon) and weak/malefic when dark.
  • Even a natural malefic can give excellent results when it owns good houses and is strong; even a benefic can disappoint when it owns difficult houses and is weak — the functional layer decides.

Further classifications

Gunas — Sun, Moon, Jupiter are Sattvic; Mercury and Venus Rajasic; Mars and Saturn (and the nodes) Tamasic — shaping disposition.

Castes of the grahas — Jupiter & Venus Brahmin; Sun royal (Kshatriya); Moon & Mercury Vaishya; Saturn Sudra; Mars Kshatriya — used in fine judgement of background and vocation.

Elements (tattva) — Mars→fire, Mercury→earth, Jupiter→ether, Venus→water, Saturn→air; the luminaries carry fire (Sun) and water (Moon).

Dhatu / Moola / Jeeva — a Prashna (horary) division: Rahu, Mars, Saturn, Moon are Dhatu (mineral); Sun and Venus are Moola (vegetable); Mercury, Jupiter, Ketu are Jeeva (animate).

Naisargika Dasa order — by natural strength the sage ranks the planets, Saturn conferring the most natural years — used in one dasha scheme (see Dashas).
