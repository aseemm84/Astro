A rāśi is a twelfth of the zodiac and nobody disputes where it starts. A bhāva is a twelfth of a life, and where one begins is a question the tradition answers in more than one way. This page draws the answers on top of each other for any moment and place, with no birth chart required, so you can see exactly where they agree, where they part, and which grahas they actually disagree about.

Three ways to cut the same circleत्रिविधा भाव-व्यवस्था

Whole sign

The rāśi holding the lagna is the first bhāva, edge to edge. The next sign is the second, and so on. House boundaries and sign boundaries are the same thing, so a graha’s sign and its house never disagree. This is the method BPHS uses throughout.

Equal from the lagna

The first bhāva begins at the lagna’s exact degree and runs 30°, and so on round. Every house is the same width, but they no longer line up with the signs — a graha can be in one sign and the next house.

Śrīpati

The lagna and the MC — the degree culminating overhead — are both fixed, and the quadrants between them are divided. The two are not 90° apart except at the equator, so the houses come out unequal, and how unequal depends on the latitude and the time of day.

All three at onceसमाक्षेपः

The outer ring is the rāśis. Inside it the three sets of house boundaries are drawn as separate rings, so where they diverge is visible directly rather than by comparing two tables.

Which grahas the systems disagree aboutभेद-सूची

This is the only question that matters in practice. Most of the time the three agree about most grahas; the interesting cases are the ones near a junction.

Why Śrīpati houses are unequalअक्षांश-प्रभावः

Drag the latitude. At the equator the quadrants are nearly even and Śrīpati is close to equal houses. Move north or south and the ecliptic meets the horizon at a steeper or shallower angle through the day, the lagna and the MC stop being a quarter-circle apart, and the houses stretch and squeeze. Past the polar circle the construction can fail outright, and the page says so rather than drawing something.

The madhya, the sandhi, and the fifteen degreesभाव-मध्य-सन्धि

Śrīpati does not only move the boundaries; it gives the inside of a bhāva a shape. The madhya is the middle of the house, where a graha gives its fullest result for that bhāva. The sandhi is the junction between two houses, where it gives none. Between the two the effect falls away across about fifteen degrees.

A weight for reading, not a score. The curve is how the Benares edition states the rule, drawn. It is not a measured quantity and nothing on this site multiplies anything by it; a graha at a sandhi is not “zero per cent” of anything, it is a graha the text says gives no result for that bhāva. Where a house is narrower than thirty degrees it reaches its sandhi before the fifteen degrees are spent, and the sandhi wins, because the text is explicit about the junction.
