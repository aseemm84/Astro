An aspect (drishti, 'glance') is how a planet or sign reaches across the chart to influence another point. BPHS carries two distinct systems. Always state which one you are applying — mixing them silently is a classic source of error.

Graha Drishti — planetary aspects

Every planet fully aspects the 7th from itself. In the graded scheme the 3rd/10th, 5th/9th and 4th/8th receive a quarter, half and three-quarter glance respectively — and three malefics upgrade one pair of these to a special full aspect.

Virupa aspect values

For strength work, BPHS converts an aspect into a number (Virupas, 60 = 1 Rupa) from the exact angular distance between aspector and aspected, with special additions for Mars, Jupiter and Saturn near their special-aspect angles. This feeds Drik Bala (see Strength); for ordinary reading, the whole-sign aspects above are enough.

Translator / commentary. Rahu and Ketu aspects are applied variously by later authors (a 5th/7th/9th scheme is common). BPHS is not emphatic here — treat node aspects cautiously and note which convention you use.

Aspects on houses

A house (its cusp) can be aspected exactly as a planet is. A benefic aspecting a house tends to protect its matters; a malefic aspecting it tends to stress them — always weighed against the house-lord's condition.

Rashi Drishti — sign aspects

Signs aspect signs by modality, regardless of longitude, and a planet inherits the aspect of the sign it occupies. Parashara states this system first; Jaimini develops it (with Arudhas and rasi dashas).

Argala — a related idea

Beyond aspect, BPHS uses Argala (intervention): planets in the 2nd, 4th, 11th (and 5th) from a point influence it, obstructed from the 12th, 10th, 3rd (and 9th). Argala is detailed on the Special Points page.

Study note. A working rule: use Graha Drishti for Parashari house/planet analysis ('what does this planet look at?'), and Rashi Drishti when you are in the Jaimini layer (Arudhas, chara karakas, sign dashas). Decide which framework the question belongs to before you draw a single line.
