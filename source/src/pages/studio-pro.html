    How this page works

    Most studio tools are a gallery: somebody writes a dozen animations and you pick one. This is not that. Every quantity the engines compute is published here with a declared shape — a longitude, a magnitude, a division of the circle, a set of pairs, an interval of time, a quantity that varies with the instant — and a small set of composers each declare which shapes they can draw.

    So a composer that draws two longitudes and the angle between them will draw the Sun and the Moon, the Lagna and the Arūḍha Lagna, a daśā lord and its dispositor, or a transiting graha against its own natal place, without knowing that any of those things exist. That is why the number of pictures this page can make is not the number of animations anyone wrote.

    When a selection is too mixed for any single view, the stage splits into panels and draws each group with whatever fits it, so there is no combination that produces nothing. And whenever a view cannot use part of what you picked, it says so on the page rather than quietly dropping it.

    Director mode

    Auto mode answers one question — what is the best picture of this selection? — and it answers it by deciding: it scores the composers, picks the winner, lays it in the one stage rectangle and times it against the one playhead. Those are the right defaults and that is exactly why they cannot also be a pro tool, because none of them is negotiable.

    Director mode takes the same composition apart into layers and hands every one of those decisions over. A layer has a what — a group of nodes and the composer that draws them, or your own text, a rule, a wash, the caption band, the stamp; a where — a rectangle in the frame, and optionally a second rectangle it travels to, which is how a move, a pan or a zoom is expressed; a when — the stretch of the reel it is on screen for; and a how — an entry, an exit, a continuous motion, an easing, and which clock the drawing inside it believes.

    Nothing in Director draws astronomy of its own. Every picture is still one of the composers above, because a composer has never been told where it sits: it is handed a rectangle and a playhead and sizes everything from them. That is why placing a diagram in a corner, holding it still while a line of text arrives, and drifting the pair of them is a matter of four fields rather than a new animation — and why a composer added for Auto mode arrives in Director already placeable, already timeable, already animatable.

    A composition is a plain object. It saves as text in this browser, reopens, and can be dropped into the storyboard as one scene among several. Text layers may carry tokens — {tithi}, {naks}, {date}, {dasha} and the rest — which resolve when the frame is drawn rather than when it was typed, so a card saved today and reopened next month states next month's almanac instead of a stale copy of today's.

    What is not here
