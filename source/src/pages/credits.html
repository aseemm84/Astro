Bundled third-party components

Four pieces of other people’s work are built into the pages. None of them is covered by the MIT grant above; each keeps its own terms.

astronomy-engine — MIT Licence, copyright © 2019–2025 Don Cross.
The ephemeris underneath every planetary position on this site. It is inlined into the chart pages with its licence header intact.
github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy
GeoNames place data — Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0).
The place search — every city name, latitude, longitude and time zone you can pick when casting a chart — is derived from the GeoNames cities15000 dataset and the GeoNames India postal extract, reshaped and compressed for search. City coordinate and time-zone data courtesy of GeoNames.
geonames.org
HYG star database — Creative Commons Attribution–ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0), compiled by David Nash from the Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star and Gliese catalogues.
The star catalogue used by the star wheel and the chart pages — the 27 yogatārās and the bright stars near the ecliptic — is derived from it: positions converted to ecliptic coordinates and sidereal longitudes, and cross-checked against Swiss Ephemeris and the star list bundled with PyEphem.
github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database
Apache ECharts 5.5.0 — Apache Licence 2.0, copyright © The Apache Software Foundation. This product includes software developed at The Apache Software Foundation.

echarts.apache.org · github.com/ecomfe/zrender

Texts this site works from

These are cited, summarised and briefly quoted for study. None of them is distributed here, and nothing on this site relicenses any of them.

  • Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra, in the English translation of R. Santhanam (Ranjan Publications). The translation, its commentary and its editorial notes are copyrighted works. This site quotes them briefly and with chapter citations; if you reuse that material, the permission under which it is quoted here does not transfer to you. For substantial reproduction, approach the publisher.
  • Śrīpatipaddhati, in the English translation of V. Subrahmanya Sastri (1937) — the source of the Śrīpati paddhati followed when that toggle is chosen, cited by adhyāya, śloka and page.
  • Pañcasiddhāntikā of Varāhamihira, in the translation of T. S. Kuppanna Sastry — the source of the Pañcasiddhāntikā tier of the Sūrya Siddhānta engine.
  • Sūrya Siddhānta — the later recension, the one Ebenezer Burgess translated, is the one the classical engine implements.

The classical Sanskrit texts themselves are ancient. Translations, commentaries and editorial apparatus are the work of their translators and editors and are credited as such wherever they are used.

What is not here

No fonts, analytics, trackers or remote services are loaded. Nothing you enter on this site is sent anywhere: the pages make no request to any other machine, and the build checks that none has crept in.
